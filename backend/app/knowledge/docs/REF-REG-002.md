## 1 About these notes
These are Syntra summary notes in our own words on the 2011 version of EU GMP Annex 11, not the
official text. Check the official EudraLex Volume 4 publication, and whether a newer version of
Annex 11 applies, before relying on a requirement.

## 2 Principle and risk management (§1)
Annex 11 applies to all computerised systems used as part of GMP-regulated activities. Risk
management is applied throughout the lifecycle, considering patient safety, data integrity and
product quality. The extent of validation and data integrity controls is based on a justified,
documented risk assessment.

## 3 Validation (§4)
Validation documentation covers the whole lifecycle. An up-to-date inventory of all relevant systems
and their GMP functionality is maintained. User requirements describe the required functions and are
traceable through the lifecycle. Evidence of appropriate test methods and scenarios is required.

## 4 Data, audit trails and security (§5, §9, §12)
Systems that exchange data electronically include checks for the correct and secure entry and
processing of data (§5). Based on risk, the system records all GMP-relevant changes and deletions in
an audit trail, with the reason documented; audit trails must be available, convertible to a
readable form and regularly reviewed (§9). Physical and logical controls restrict access to
authorised persons, and the creation, change and cancellation of access authorisations is recorded
(§12).

## 5 Change and configuration management (§10)
Any change to a computerised system, including configuration, is made only in a controlled manner
according to a defined procedure.

## 6 Periodic evaluation (§11)
Computerised systems are periodically evaluated to confirm that they remain in a valid state and
compliant with GMP. The evaluation covers functionality, deviation records, incidents, problems,
upgrade history, performance, reliability, security and validation status reports.

## 7 Incident management (§13)
All incidents, not only system failures and data errors, are reported and assessed. The root cause of
a critical incident is identified and forms the basis of corrective and preventive actions.

## 8 Electronic signatures (§14)
Electronic signatures have the same impact as handwritten signatures within the company, are
permanently linked to their record, and include the time and date they were applied.

## 9 Business continuity (§16)
For systems supporting critical processes, provisions ensure continuity in case of a breakdown, for
example a manual or alternative system. The time needed to bring alternatives into use is based on
risk, and the arrangements are documented and tested.
