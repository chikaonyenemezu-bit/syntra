## 1 About these notes
These are Syntra summary notes in our own words, not the official text. 21 CFR Part 11 is the US FDA
regulation on electronic records and electronic signatures. Always check the official text at the
source above before relying on a requirement.

## 2 Scope (§11.1)
Part 11 applies to records in electronic form that are created, modified, maintained, archived,
retrieved or transmitted under FDA record requirements, and to electronic signatures executed on them.

## 3 Controls for closed systems (§11.10)
Systems must have procedures and controls that ensure authenticity, integrity and, where
appropriate, confidentiality of records, including:
- validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability and consistent intended performance (a),
- the ability to generate accurate and complete copies for inspection (b),
- protection of records so they can be retrieved throughout the retention period (c),
- limiting system access to authorised individuals (d),
- secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails that record the date and time of operator
  entries and actions that create, modify or delete records; changes must not obscure previously
  recorded information, and audit trails are retained at least as long as the records (e),
- operational checks to enforce permitted sequencing of steps (f) and authority checks so that only
  authorised individuals can sign or perform operations (g),
- training of the people who develop, maintain or use the systems (i), and written policies that
  hold individuals accountable for actions taken under their electronic signatures (j),
- control of system documentation (k).

## 4 Signature manifestations and linking (§11.50, §11.70)
Signed electronic records must show the printed name of the signer, the date and time of signing,
and the meaning of the signature, such as review, approval, responsibility or authorship. Signatures
must be linked to their records so that they cannot be removed, copied or transferred to falsify a
record.

## 5 Electronic signature requirements (§11.100, §11.200)
Each electronic signature is unique to one individual and is not reused by or reassigned to anyone
else. The organisation verifies the identity of the individual before assigning a signature.
Signatures not based on biometrics use at least two distinct components, such as a user ID and a
password. During one continuous session, the first signing uses all components and later signings
use at least one component that only the individual can execute, such as the password.

## 6 Controls for identification codes and passwords (§11.300)
Controls include keeping each combination of ID and password unique, periodically checking and
revising passwords, procedures for lost or compromised credentials, and safeguards that detect and
report attempts at unauthorised use.
